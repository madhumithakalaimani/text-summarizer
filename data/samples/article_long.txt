The city council approved a plan on Tuesday to build 40 kilometres of 
new protected bike lanes over the next three years. Officials said the
 project aims to reduce traffic congestion and improve safety for cyclists
  across the downtown area. The plan will cost about 12 million dollars,
   most of which will come from a regional transport grant. Supporters argue
    that safer bike lanes will encourage more residents to cycle to work and 
    school. A survey last year found that nearly half of residents would cycle
     more often if dedicated lanes were available. Local business owners have 
     expressed mixed reactions to the proposal. Some shop owners worry that 
     removing street parking will hurt sales, while others expect more foot 
     traffic from cyclists. The council promised to hold public meetings in 
     every district before construction begins. Engineers will start with the
      busiest corridors, where accidents involving cyclists have been most common.
       Similar projects in other cities have led to a drop in serious injuries 
       and a rise in daily cycling trips. Critics have questioned whether the budget
        will be enough, pointing to cost overruns on earlier road projects. The mayor
         said the city will publish quarterly progress reports so residents can track
          spending and delivery.
